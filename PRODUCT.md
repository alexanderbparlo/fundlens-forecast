# FundLens Forecast — Product Context

## Product
FundLens Forecast is a GP-facing scenario analysis and forward-looking return projection tool for alternative investment fund managers. It is part of the DeciFin / FundLens suite and serves as the design benchmark for the suite going forward — other suite tools will align to the visual and interaction standard set here.

## Register
product — design serves the tool. The intake flow (fund type selection, upload, confirmation) warrants brand-level craft. The analysis and results surface is a precision decision-support instrument.

## Users
Decision-makers at alternative asset funds: solo General Partners at emerging managers, and CFOs / COOs at mid-to-large funds. They make high-stakes investment and operational decisions daily. They are sophisticated, time-constrained, and data-literate — but not necessarily technical.

They need information that is comprehensive, detailed, relevant, and reliable. Anything that undermines that trust — missing data, vague outputs, lite-feeling UI — is a failure.

**When they open this tool they feel:**
- Well-informed and confident — ready to make a decision, not still gathering data
- In control — the complexity of multi-scenario analysis feels manageable, not overwhelming
- Ahead of the curve — this represents a shift from manual Excel-based analysis to AI-assisted projection

**Over time they feel:**
- Relieved — hundreds of hours of manual analysis eliminated
- Optimistic — the fund is moving in the right direction operationally
- Validated — they made the right call adopting this before the rest of the market did

## Tone
Confident. Precise. Premium. Authoritative without being cold.

- Never hedging, never apologetic, never "lite"
- Data is presented with conviction — caveats are stated once and clearly, not repeated
- UI copy sounds like a trusted senior analyst, not a product tour
- Sophisticated enough for the user, accessible enough to not require a manual
- No jargon without context, but no dumbing down either

## Anti-References
- **Bloomberg Terminal** — dense, monochrome, dated. Built for traders, not fund managers. This user should never feel like they're navigating a terminal.
- **Excel glorified** — flat, tabular, no visual hierarchy, no intelligence. If it looks like a spreadsheet with a paint job, it has failed.
- **Generic SaaS dashboard** — card grids, hero metric templates with gradient accents, identical layouts, trial-version energy, indistinguishable from 50 other tools.
- **Early 2000s fintech** — retrofitted, behind the curve. This communicates the opposite of innovation to a user who sees themselves as ahead of it.

## Strategic Principles
1. **Data-rich, not data-dense** — hierarchy solves complexity. Never reduce data to solve density; restructure and prioritize instead.
2. **Every element earns its place** — no decorative stats, no filler content, no icon for every label, no padding that exists to look busy.
3. **Information feels alive** — transitions, state changes, and loading moments communicate intelligence, not lag. The results screen should feel like data becoming visible, not a page loading.
4. **Built for this specific user** — nothing generic, nothing adapted from a B2C context. Design decisions should only make sense for a GP.
5. **Confidence without excess** — premium feels restrained and precise, not loud or ornate.
6. **Optionality without compromise** — adaptive theme means both light and dark are first-class. Light is the presentation mode; dark is the analysis mode. Neither is an afterthought.
